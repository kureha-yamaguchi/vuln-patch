## The categories, fixed now

A dismissal on an *overfit* leg is **not automatically wrong**. The leg was
missed, but the specific check may genuinely have been unsound — dismissing an
unsound check is correct behaviour that happens to coincide with a miss. The
category list must let that outcome win, or the study will manufacture a lever
out of correct judgments.

| # | category | meaning | is the dismissal right? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **grounded refutation** | the dismissal quotes shown material that genuinely shows the check is unsound | **YES — on the merits** |
| 2 | **wrong-family** | the check could not have convicted this bug regardless; its dismissal is irrelevant to the miss | **YES — on the merits** (the miss is upstream) |
| 3 | **near-tolerance / numeric margin** | dismissed because the observed difference sits within or near a tolerance | **contested** — merited if the tolerance is the real contract, drift if not |
| 4 | **setup divergence** | dismissed because the harness's setup differs from the test's, not because the property is false | **contested** |
| 5 | **ungrounded hypothetical** | "a correct implementation could …" with no citation — the measured drift-kill signature | **NO — this is the lever, if one exists** |

**Only category 5 licenses a mechanism.** Categories 1 and 2 are correct
judgments; 3 and 4 are contested and would need their own evidence. A study that
finds mostly 1s and 2s has found that the judge class is *not* a lever — and that
is a legitimate, honest close, not a failure to look hard enough.
